QUESTIONS I KEEP COMING BACK TO

Interests.

Intelligence, biology, and the choices we make around technology.

I’m interested in building systems that can understand and interact with the world. Biology and healthcare are especially exciting places to explore that, but my curiosity also extends to how we think, make decisions, and live.

Intelligence in the physical world

I want to understand both the hardware and software behind AI. Robotics and autonomous systems interest me because they connect computation to sensing, movement, and decisions in the real world.

AI for scientific discovery

I’m excited by systems that propose experiments, help carry them out, and learn from the results. Autonomous labs bring together software, robotics, and biology in a way I’d like to work on.

Digital twins & computational biology

My research in gene expression and microRNA regulation has made me curious about how we model complex biological systems. I’m interested in digital twins, molecular design, and how predictions can guide useful experiments, diagnostics, and personalized medicine.

Brains, biology & computing

Brain–computer interfaces, living-neuron computing, and biological circuits make me curious about the relationship between intelligence and its physical form. What can we learn from biological systems, and how might we connect them with electronics?

AI, philosophy & people

I keep coming back to questions about intelligence, consciousness, and human behavior. I’m also interested in how we evaluate AI, understand its risks, and develop policies around it—and how we decide what we actually want technology to do for us.

Building something useful

I’m drawn to entrepreneurship and the work of turning research into tools people can use. I’m curious about what small teams can build with AI and experimental partners, and I like working with people who see a problem differently.
